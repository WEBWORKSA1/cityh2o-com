A **boil-water advisory** (or notice) means your utility can't guarantee the water is free of disease-causing microbes — often after a main break, a loss of pressure, a treatment problem or a positive bacteria test. It's usually precautionary, but you should follow it until it's lifted.

> Advisories differ from **"do not drink"** or **"do not use"** notices, which are issued for chemical contamination. Boiling does **not** make chemically contaminated water safe. Read your notice carefully.

## How to boil water correctly

1. If the water is cloudy, let it settle and draw off the clear water, or filter it through a clean cloth or coffee filter.
2. Bring it to a **rolling boil for 1 minute**. At elevations above **6,500 feet (about 2,000 metres)**, boil for **3 minutes**.
3. Let it cool, then store it in clean, covered containers.
4. To improve flat taste, pour it between two clean containers a few times.

Electric kettles with automatic shut-off are fine if they reach a full rolling boil.

## What's safe and what isn't

| Activity | During the advisory |
|---|---|
| Drinking, cooking, coffee, ice | Boiled (then cooled) or bottled water only. Throw out ice made after the problem began. |
| Baby formula | Boiled water cooled to a safe temperature, or bottled water labelled suitable for infants. |
| Brushing teeth | Boiled or bottled water. |
| Washing fruit and vegetables | Boiled or bottled water. |
| Showering and bathing | Generally OK for adults — don't swallow. Sponge-bathe babies and young children. |
| Washing hands | Soap and tap water is usually fine; use hand sanitiser afterwards if advised. |
| Dishes | A dishwasher with a hot dry cycle or sanitising setting is usually fine. Hand-washing: rinse in water with a small amount of unscented bleach, per your health department's instructions. |
| Pets | Give pets boiled (cooled) or bottled water. |

## Filters during an advisory

Most pitcher, faucet and fridge filters are **not** designed to remove bacteria and viruses. Unless a product is certified for microbiological reduction (for example NSF/ANSI 55 Class A UV or an equivalent certified purifier), **keep boiling**.

## When the advisory is lifted

1. Run all **cold water taps** for a few minutes, starting at the top floor, until the water runs cold.
2. Flush fridge water lines and discard the first three batches of ice.
3. **Replace filter cartridges** that were used with untreated water (fridge, pitcher, under-sink).
4. Run water softeners and RO systems through a regeneration or flush per the manufacturer.
5. Drain and refill your water heater if your utility recommends it (often only after long advisories).

## Who's most at risk

Infants, older adults, pregnant people and anyone with a weakened immune system should be most careful. If someone develops diarrhoea, vomiting or fever during an advisory, contact a healthcare provider.
