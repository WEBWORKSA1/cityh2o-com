PFAS — per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances — are a family of thousands of synthetic chemicals used since the 1940s in non-stick coatings, stain- and water-repellent fabrics, food packaging and firefighting foam. Their carbon-fluorine bonds are among the strongest in chemistry, which is why they're called **"forever chemicals"**: they barely break down in the environment or the body.

## Where the rules stand (November 2026)

In April 2024, the U.S. EPA set the first enforceable national drinking water limits for PFAS:

| Compound | 2024 limit | Status after May 2026 proposals |
|---|---|---|
| **PFOA** | 4 ppt | **Kept.** EPA proposed letting qualifying systems apply for up to two extra years (deadline from 2029 to 2031). |
| **PFOS** | 4 ppt | **Kept**, same extension pathway. |
| PFHxS, PFNA, HFPO-DA ("GenX") | 10 ppt each | EPA has proposed rescinding these limits. |
| Mixtures (Hazard Index) | HI of 1 | EPA has proposed rescinding. |

In short: **the 4 ppt limits for PFOA and PFOS remain the law**, and the original compliance date of 2029 still applies unless a system receives an extension. Under the proposal, systems seeking an extension that have PFOA or PFOS above 12 ppt would have to take short-term steps to reduce exposure in the meantime. Several states have their own, sometimes stricter, PFAS standards.

To picture **4 parts per trillion**: it's roughly four drops of water in twenty Olympic-sized swimming pools. Limits are set that low because these compounds build up in the body over years.

In Canada, Health Canada set an **objective of 30 ng/L (ppt) for the sum of 25 PFAS** in 2024.

## Why it matters

Long-term exposure to PFOA and PFOS has been linked to certain cancers (including kidney and testicular), immune system effects including reduced vaccine response, thyroid disease, higher cholesterol, and developmental effects. Drinking water is only one exposure route, but it's the one you can control most easily.

## How PFAS get into tap water

- **Firefighting foam** used at airports, military bases and fire-training sites, which soaks into groundwater.
- **Industrial discharges** from manufacturers that made or used PFAS.
- **Landfills and wastewater** — conventional treatment plants don't remove PFAS.
- **Biosolids** spread on farmland.

Groundwater near these sources is most at risk, but surface water downstream can be affected too.

## How to check your water

1. **Read your utility's water quality report.** Look for PFOA and PFOS in the regulated table and the UCMR 5 section, which covered 29 PFAS. See [how to read it](/guides/how-to-read-your-water-quality-report.html).
2. **Check state data.** Many states publish PFAS sampling results by water system.
3. **Use the USGS PFAS tap water map** to see modelled risk where you live. Watch the [USGS walkthrough](/videos.html).
4. **Test.** PFAS tests use specialised methods (EPA 533 or 537.1) and cost more than basic tests — typically $150–$500 for a meaningful panel through an accredited lab. Results let you compare directly with the 4 ppt limits.
5. **Private wells** near a known source should always be tested — no utility will do it for you.

## Filters that work

Not every filter removes PFAS. Look for a certification that **specifically lists PFOA and PFOS reduction**:

| Technology | PFAS performance | Certification to look for |
|---|---|---|
| **Reverse osmosis (RO)** | Most reliable; removes long- and short-chain PFAS | NSF/ANSI 58 with PFOA/PFOS claim |
| **Carbon block** (under-sink, some faucet and pitcher models) | Good for PFOA/PFOS when certified; less consistent for short-chain PFAS | NSF/ANSI 53 with PFOA/PFOS claim |
| **Ion exchange (specialised resins)** | Effective, often used with carbon | Check the specific listing |
| Basic carbon pitchers *without* a PFAS claim | Not proven | — |
| Boiling | **Doesn't work** — concentrates PFAS | — |

Two rules make or break PFAS filtration: **only trust the certification listing** (not the box), and **change cartridges on schedule**, because saturated carbon stops capturing PFAS.

## What about showering?

The main exposure route is drinking and cooking. Skin absorption from bathing is considered minor for PFOA and PFOS, so most households focus filtration on drinking water.

## Bottom line

The national 4 ppt limits for PFOA and PFOS are staying; what's moving is the timeline. Find your utility's PFAS results, and if PFOA or PFOS are detected, a certified RO system or certified carbon block filter is the most effective step you can take today.
