FAQ

Common tap water questions

Is my city's tap water safe to drink?

Most public water systems in the U.S. and Canada meet national standards most of the time. Problems usually come from your own plumbing — lead service lines, lead solder, old brass fixtures — or from contaminants with new or evolving limits such as PFAS. Read your utility's annual water quality report, and test at your own tap if you live in an older home or have an infant.

How do I find my water quality report?

U.S. utilities must deliver a Consumer Confidence Report (CCR) by July 1 each year. Search for your utility name plus "water quality report," check the back of your bill, or use the EPA's Find Your CCR tool. Our CCR reading guide explains every column.

What filter removes lead and PFAS?

Look for a filter certified to NSF/ANSI 53 for lead, and NSF/ANSI 53 or 58 with a specific PFOA/PFOS claim for PFAS. Reverse osmosis systems certified to NSF/ANSI 58 cover the widest range. Try the Filter Finder.

Is hard water bad for me?

No — calcium and magnesium aren't a health risk. Hard water costs you money through scale in water heaters, dishwashers and fixtures. See our hard water guide and softener payback calculator.

Does CityH2O sell filters?

No. We're an independent information site supported by advertising, sponsorships and reader donations. When you request a water check we may, with your permission, introduce you to a certified local professional. See our editorial policy.
